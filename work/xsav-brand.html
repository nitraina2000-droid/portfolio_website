Brand case study · XSAV Lab

Rebuilding XSAV Lab's brand

XSAV Lab is a Pune-based tech agency that offers cybersecurity, cloud engineering, AI agents and custom software. Its brand, though, only talked about security, and its old logo didn't match the rest of its look. During my internship, I rebuilt the brand: a clear message, a new logo, simple rules for how it looks and sounds, and the sales and social material to go with it.

01
The problem

One company, three different impressions

When I joined in July 2026, I read everything XSAV had published: its website, LinkedIn posts and sales documents. The same company came across differently in each place.

What it said

The website described XSAV only as a cybersecurity company, leaving out its cloud, AI and software work.

How it looked

The old hexagon logo used teal and grey, colours that appeared nowhere else on the website.

How it sounded

Words like "cutting-edge" and "leverage" made XSAV sound like any other IT company.

Before designing anything, I needed to agree on what the brand should actually say.
02
The approach

Four steps, from listening to sign-off

I framed the work around three questions: what do we say, how do we look, and how do we sound?

Step 1

Listen first

Reviewed the website, social posts, sales decks and a few competitors, and noted every inconsistency.

Step 2

Define the message

Wrote one sentence about what XSAV does, with three key messages to support it.

Step 3

Design the identity

Designed a new logo and chose the colours, font and tone to go with it.

Step 4

Get sign-off

Presented the new brand to the CEO, who approved it.

Why this message

Businesses often juggle separate vendors for security, cloud and AI. XSAV's strength is doing all of it with one team, so the message had to cover everything, not just security.

03
The new brand

A brand that holds together

Three pieces work together: what XSAV says, how it looks, and the rules that keep both consistent.

The message

"We keep businesses secure, run them on the cloud and put AI agents to work for them."

Tagline: Innovate with AI. Secure with confidence.

The logo

The old logo felt disconnected from the rest of the brand, so I designed a simpler mark in its place: four identical triangles, all leaning the same way, like a team moving in step towards one goal.

WhiteThe main version, for dark backgrounds like the website.
NavyFor white pages, documents and print.
GoldFor special print, such as business cards.

What changed

BeforeAfter
What we say"A cybersecurity company"One line that covers all of XSAV's services
How we lookA teal and grey hexagon that didn't match the websiteA new four-triangle logo with six colours that match it

The rules, written down

I put everything into a short brand guide, so anyone at XSAV can use the brand the same way.

How I used AI. Claude helped me draft faster and Gemini helped with visuals. I decided what went in, checked every page and removed anything the team wouldn't use.

05
Reflection

What I'd take forward

The new brand is still in use across XSAV's website, sales decks and LinkedIn, and it's shared with clients and new joiners.

  1. 01
    Keep it short

    A guide only works if people actually use it, so I kept it to the essentials: message, logo, colours, font and tone.

  2. 02
    Track results from day one

    I didn't track reach on the LinkedIn posts, so I can't show which ones worked best. Next time, I'd set up simple tracking before the first post goes live.

  3. 03
    Follow your own rules

    Some of my own carousels drifted from the brand colours. Consistency has to start with the person who writes the rules.
