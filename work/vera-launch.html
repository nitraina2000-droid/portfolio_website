A go-to-market plan for VERA

VERA by Kamakshi curates luxury bags from around the world. I built the plan for its online launch: who to sell to, how to position it, how to price and promote it, and how to launch without wasting the budget.

Role
Marketing Intern
Brand
VERA by Kamakshi
When
Aug to Sep 2026
Outcome
Plan approved by the CEO

The challenge

Launch a new luxury brand online with a growing catalogue and no sales history to guide the spend.

My approach

Define the customer and positioning, set the marketing mix, map ads to the funnel, then test before scaling.

The outcome

The CEO approved the plan. My internship ended before launch, so there are no results yet.

i
The brief

A new brand, a growing catalogue, no data yet

VERA sells evening bags and clutches that it hand-picks from suppliers abroad. The CEO asked me for a go-to-market plan for its online store, using the product photos and a working version of the website he shared.

Two things shaped the plan. The catalogue was growing towards about 90 products, and with no sales history, nobody knew yet which of them people would actually buy.

ii
The customer

Segment, target, position

Before any ad, I worked out who VERA is for and why she should choose it.

Primary segment

The occasion shopper

Buys for weddings, festivals and celebrations. She wants to look elevated at events, several times a year, and the bag has to work with Indian and western outfits.

Secondary segment

The everyday indulger

Buys one elegant piece for daily use as a personal treat, not tied to an occasion. Worth growing into as the catalogue widens.

The festive shopper

22 to 32, urban India, Instagram-native. She attends several weddings and celebrations a year and wants to look elegant without paying luxury-label prices. Three doubts stand between her and checkout:

"Will it go with my outfit?"
"Is it worth the money?"
"Can I trust a brand I've never heard of?"
Positioning statement

For young Indian women who want to look effortlessly elegant at weddings and celebrations, VERA is the accessible-luxury bag label whose every piece is hand-picked from around the world, so they get international design without luxury-label prices.

CuratedSelected from global suppliers, not mass-listed
Accessible luxuryPremium look, priced for real life
Occasion-readyMade for the moments she dresses up for
iii
Marketing mix

The four Ps, built for luxury

Each part of the mix had to support one idea: a premium brand that feels worth buying from, even though it's new.

P

Product

A curated catalogue of about 90 pieces, shown with one consistent image style so the store looks like one brand. Unboxing is treated as part of the product: considered packaging and a handwritten note.

P

Price

Accessible luxury: premium, but within reach for her occasions. No discount codes, because a coupon tells a luxury buyer the price was never real. Value comes from free shipping, gift wrap and a care kit instead.

P

Place

Direct to consumer, through VERA's own website. Shoppers arrive from Meta and Google, and an email and WhatsApp list is built from day one so VERA isn't fully dependent on paid traffic.

P

Promotion

Paid ads mapped to each stage of the funnel, organic Instagram, the founder's own network, and later a press kit and a few well-matched micro-influencers.

The funnel

A different reason at every stage

Someone seeing VERA for the first time needs a reason to notice it. Someone ready to buy needs a reason to act now. I designed twelve ads and mapped each one to a stage of the funnel.

Get noticed

For people who've never heard of VERA. Brand and mood first, with a soft call to action.

Answer one doubt each

For people who know VERA but aren't sure yet. Each ad answers one of the festive shopper's three doubts.

Give a reason to act now

For people ready to buy, and for those who visited and left without buying.

v
The roadmap

Test, learn, then scale

With about 90 products and no sales history, picking a "hero" product upfront would have been a guess. So the plan lets real customer data choose the winners, and only then puts serious money behind them.

What happens

Set up sales tracking and the product feed, so every rupee spent later can be measured. No ad spend in this phase.

Ready to move on when

Tracking is verified end to end and the catalogue is approved for Google Shopping.

What happens

Meta and Google catalogue ads show the whole range with a small budget, while organic Instagram and the founder's network run alongside.

Ready to move on when

There's enough traffic to learn from, a baseline cost per customer, and the first genuine reviews.

What happens

Read the data, shortlist the three to five products people actually buy, and invest in premium content and retargeting for those only.

Ready to move on when

The winning products return more than they cost in ads, and real reviews are building up.

What happens

Move most of the budget to the proven winners for the festive season, add a simple loyalty perk and post-purchase emails, and reach out to press and micro-influencers.

Ready to move on when

Customers come back to buy again, and festive revenue grows at a cost per customer VERA can sustain.

“

No revenue target before tracking exists. Each phase earns the next with one measurable test.

vi
Reflection

What I'd take forward

I presented the plan to the CEO, who liked it. My internship ended before launch, and I don't know whether it has been put into action, so there are no results to share. These are the lessons I'm keeping.

  1. i
    Let data pick the winners

    With a large catalogue and no history, testing before investing is cheaper and more reliable than backing one product on instinct.

  2. ii
    Every part of the mix tells the brand story

    In luxury, a discount code or a careless unboxing can undo what the ads build. Price and packaging are marketing too.

  3. iii
    Measure before you spend

    Clear exit tests for each phase made the plan easy to approve, because the CEO could see exactly when money would be committed and why.
